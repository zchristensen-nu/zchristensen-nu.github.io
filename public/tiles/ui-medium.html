Editor’s letter

September 2026

Workbench began as a folder called misc. Every project I finished ended up there, next to the ones I didn’t, and after a year the folder said more about my habits than my work. So I started writing up the finished ones.

The rule is simple: nothing goes here until it has been used by someone other than me for at least a week. That keeps the essays honest. They are about what happened, not what I hoped would happen, and they tend to be most useful in the paragraphs where something broke.

This fall there are three projects on the bench. One puts review comments directly on a prototype. One reads the news so a small group of people can start the day already briefed. And one is the website you are looking at, drawn five different ways so I can argue with each of them.

Thank you for reading, and for the notes. Keep sending them. They are the best part of shipping in public.

Z.Editor, Workbench

Z. Christensen

I build small tools that make reviewing and shipping software less lossy, and I write about them once they have survived contact with other people.

Lately that means three things: claude-feedback, a proxy that lets reviewers pin comments directly onto local prototypes; Morning Briefing, an agent that reads the news each morning and writes a digest people actually finish; and Style Tiles, the design exploration you are browsing right now.

Before that I spent a long time on product teams, mostly in the gap between a design file and the thing that ships. I still think that gap is where most of the interesting problems live.
